MARK: Yeah, that can be daunting. Another thing is that we all make plenty of choices as consumers. But selecting a college– this is an especially tricky choice. This is different than choosing what you want off the menu at Chipotle or picking a new vacuum–

JOHN: Indeed, yes.

HANNAH: Yes, if you are lucky enough to visit campus on a day when John is giving an information session, you’ll hear his advice for starting the college process with a list of non-negotiables. But we thought that everyone should have the opportunity to hear this little talk of yours.

MARK: So tell us what it means when you talk about non-negotiables, John?

JOHN: Sure. I think one of the difficult parts about the college search process is there’s a lot of what I’ll call outside noise. There’s a lot of people who ultimately aren’t you telling you what they think you should want, what they think you think you want, what the world generally should want you to want. In other words, there’s a lot of people that have opinions about what your college experience should be, and those are all valid opinions, but they’re not yours. And yours is what matters.

So whenever I have the opportunity to talk to prospective students and families about the college search process, I first acknowledging the fact that it is overwhelming. There are so many choices to make. So as opposed to taking in all of the thousands of different colleges and universities that are out there, it can be helpful to actually start with yourself and just ask yourself, what am I looking for in a college experience? And most importantly, what are my non-negotiables?

In other words, you’re going to spend four years of your life as part of a college community, and there are going to be a list of things that you’re looking for from a college experience. But there are especially going to be a few factors that I would call non-negotiables that are very important. And so if colleges and universities do not have this facet to their community or do not offer you that experience, then it’s not going to be a school that necessarily you’re going to be interested in.

Now, what I like about the non-negotiables framework is that you ultimately can sit there and decide what a non-negotiable is for you. So for some of you, it might be location. If you’re at home thinking my dream college experience is living this thoreauvian fantasy of reading books in the middle of the woods, that’s fantastic. That’s awesome.

And if being in a rural location is a non-negotiable for you, then it’s really helpful to lean into that. Because then you know I’m not interested in schools located in an urban environment. A non-negotiable for you have to do with academics. Maybe you have your heart set on studying quantum mechanics, or you’re specifically interested in a program that asks you to read a high volume of great books.

If that’s a non-negotiable for you, then you can start to narrow down your list of colleges and really isolate those schools, all of which have a variety of admissions rates. That offer you that experience.

And so personally, I know for me, I grew up in Central Jersey. I love the idea of being able to take a train home, and that was really important to me. Call me a mama’s boy, but it was very important to me. So I really isolated most of my college list to the northeast.

Another non-negotiable for me was a university that had a really thriving and world class classical music scene. I was an avid flutist growing up. I also sang a cappella in high school, and I knew that a non-negotiable was a college that offered, in its undergraduate scene, a very robust music scene. And some of the non-negotiables can feel a little personal as well.

I knew for me, a thriving LGBTQ community was really, really important. Yale has a fantastic reputation for being a very welcoming and supportive community for queer students. That’s something that I got to confirm when I visited campus. And so before you even start thinking about the kinds of schools that you can look at, come up with a list of things that are your non-negotiables– be it the location, the academic programming, the community. And then once you have those list of factors that are so important to you, you don’t want to sacrifice them, then you can jump off from there.

MARK: Yeah.

HANNAH: And you just know what you’re looking for when you start reading those brochures or visiting campuses if you have taken the time to define those non-negotiables for yourself.

JOHN: Right. And I want to encourage listeners to feel like their non-negotiables can be anything.

MARK: Yeah.

JOHN: The weather is a thing.

HANNAH: Yeah.

JOHN: It’s important. And if the idea of going to college in four seasons is not appealing to you so much to the point that it’s a non-negotiable, maybe applying to schools in New England isn’t in your cards.

MARK: So, John, I’m curious how you would advise a student to differentiate those things that they know about themselves, and, specifically, that they know that they want in college from the things where they say, well, gosh, I don’t know. Either I don’t know what’s going to be important to me when I’m in college or gosh, I’ve not been to college.

I don’t really know what the difference between this residential environment or that dorm situation is. I don’t know what I want. How should they navigate that?

JOHN: That’s a great question, and that’s why I like that we acknowledge, at the start, this is going to take some homework and this is going to take some introspection. If you aren’t really sure about something or are undecided, maybe you’re not really sure how you feel about the weather, or maybe you’re not exactly sure what academic programming you’re looking for, then that’s not a non-negotiable. That’s just a factor that you’re relatively flexible on. But you’d be hard pressed to tell me there isn’t a factor or a series of factors that you aren’t sure about.

MARK: Absolutely.

JOHN: So I would advise spending less time worrying about the things you’re not sure of and actually spend time reflecting on the things you are sure of.

HANNAH: John, I’m curious what you would say about how students should establish the right timeline for their non-negotiables or their aspirations in college. Should they be thinking at this point, short-term, medium-term, long-term? So like, I want to enroll in a school that has this club that would be a more short-term thing versus more longer term. Like I want to be able to land a job with a top international management consulting firm once I graduate in four years.

JOHN: I think when you’re thinking about colleges and asking these questions, it can be very tempting to start spiraling and thinking way too far in the future.

MARK: Yes.

JOHN: And my best advice would be your non-negotiables should ideally have to do with the environment that is curated by the school, as opposed to a specific end goal down the line. Because if you land at a college or university that meets your environment that you’re looking for, then you’re going to thrive, you’re going to be happy, and you’re going to have the resources to reach that end goal.

So as opposed to thinking, my non-negotiable is that I want to have this kind of job. I think a better way to frame it is I’m going to get that kind of job if I’m in a community that offers this kind of experience.

MARK: I think that’s really well said. And I think that tees us up for some of the final thoughts that we wanted to share with our listeners. I understand that for some of you, this whole process might sound a little bit celly of the look inward and know thyself. And it might also come across to you like, well, hey, that’s a luxury that some students from a certain socioeconomic background have.

The stakes for me might seem higher than it is for somebody else.

And I think, John, what you were saying is what we want to reinforce to our listeners. Even though that reasoning makes sense, john, I think your advice still applies.

JOHN: Yeah, and I think our advice still applies specifically because this non-negotiables framework that we talked about helps, I think, students realize that it’s not about going to a specific school or a school with a particularly low admission rate, and rather realizing that there are so many different schools out there that can offer you the experience you’re looking for. If I had a conversation with you and you said my non-negotiable is that I attend a school with an admit rate below x percent.

MARK: Yeah.

JOHN: Then I would ask you to maybe ask more values-based questions because that admit rate has nothing to do with the community that’s being offered, the location, the academic programming, the extracurricular opportunities. So when students start thinking more of wholly about the experience they’re going to have and then start looking for schools that offer that experience, one thing they’ll find is that they realize, wow, I can have this ideal college experience at a whole host of schools, all of which match my non-negotiables but have varying rates of admission.

And when you apply to that suite of schools, as opposed to just the small few with a very low admissions rate, you’re going to be gaining admission to one of those schools. And then it’s been a successful process because you have leaned into what’s important to you. You have applied to schools that only match those qualities, and then you end up enrolling at a school because you’ve done your work and you know you’re going to be happy there, as opposed to leaning into a ranking or an admission rate.

HANNAH: It can be, I think, really tempting to just say, I want the best– the best college, the best grad school, the highest paying job, all of those things.
